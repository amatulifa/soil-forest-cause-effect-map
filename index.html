How to use it, and its limits

Reading the arrows

Every box is a quantity that can go up or down. A blue arrow means “more of this gives more of that”, a dashed red arrow “more of this gives less of that”, and a dotted grey arrow means the direction depends on conditions. Signs multiply along a chain: two minus arrows in a row give a plus.

The tools

Effects follows a change in the chosen box along every chain of arrows and marks each box it reaches with ↑ (all chains raise it), ↓ (all lower it) or ± (chains disagree, or one passes a grey arrow). Causes runs the other way and finds everything that raises or lowers the box. Trace lists every chain between two boxes and explains each step. Feedback loops lists chains that return to their start: reinforcing loops (R) amplify a change, balancing loops (B) oppose it.

Click a result to see its chains, click an arrow for its mechanism and sources, and hide categories to simplify the map: hidden boxes are also left out of every chain. Copy link saves the current view in the address, so you can send exactly what you see.

What it can and cannot tell you

The map shows directions, not sizes. When chains disagree, the signs alone cannot say which wins; that needs measurements or a quantitative model. Longer chains are usually weaker, because each step passes on only part of the change, so the map starts with chains of up to 4 arrows and says which way the most direct chains point. You can allow up to 8. Arrows also run on very different time scales: interception changes within minutes, base saturation over decades, podzolisation over centuries.

Most arrows hold for temperate and boreal forests on mineral soils. Where an arrow holds only under certain conditions, its note says so. Some links that are real but weak or disputed are left out to keep the map readable, and it does not cover peat soils, fire or groundwater-affected soils.

Use it to think through a question, plan what to measure and explain results. It is not a substitute for a model or for local data.

Adding or changing an arrow

All boxes, arrows and sources are in one file, data/network.js. Add an entry there and it appears on the map, in every tool and in the tables. Suggestions with a source are welcome as an issue or pull request on GitHub.
